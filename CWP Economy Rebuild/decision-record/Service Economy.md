# Urbanization
Urbanization, due to its relatively adstract nature, is not best represented by just assigning an urbanization value to a building group, though we can assign each building its own urbanization value by giving each building its own building group. Urbanization would be better represented via the Production Methods, as the number of jobs as well as the input goods/output goods give a better representation of how much urbanization such a building should create. Theoretically, it would be possible to do this in the production methods in the future if Paradox ever add the ability to add urbanization to the production methods; until then, we will have to work on the state level using the goods produced and consumed in a state, as well as the job types within that state.

Each job type a production method uses contributes a certain amount of urbanization dependent on how much that job type would contribute to urban settlement; with job types concentrated in cities contributing the most urbanization to the state, while rural job types contribute little to zero urbanization to the state. # Urbanization: Weighting

All three sources of urbanization need to be balanced against the other to ensure that this urbanization calculation produces intuitive outcomes. As such, the recommendation would be to use the following weighting as a starting point:
- Job Type: 60%
- Input Goods: 20%
- Output Goods: 20%

This weighting is designed to avoid "double counting" industrialization too heavily while still allowing production methods to noticeably affect the urban growth of a state, while also taking into account that cities are ultimately collections of people rather than collections of goods. This should produce intuitive outcomes such as:
- A textile manufactory urbanizes because it employs machinists and engineers, consumes industrial inputs like chemicals and electricity, and produces manufactured goods - clothes
- A wheat farm has many farmers, consumes mostly rural inputs, and produces grain, so it contributes very little urbanization
- Banks and skyscrapers produce deposits and professional services with many clerks, shopkeepers, and engineers, so it becomes one of the strongest sources of urbanization despite having minimal heavy industry
- Oil rigs contribute modestly through its workforce and extractive output, but an oil refinery processing that oil into refined fuel contributes much more urbanization because refining is a substantially more urban and industrial activity

Due to jobs and the input/output goods being on completely different scales - thousands of jobs compared to tens/hundreds of goods - the urbanization value from job type will dominate by 1 or 2 orders of magnitude if not accounted for; however, considering the service economy's size and scale as well as the nature of urbanization, it would be preferrable to make the job type urbanization slightly more important. Furthermore, large-scale heavy industry becomes disproportionately urbanizing, so the urbanization from industrial production methods should create a greater increase in urbanization than farms and plantations. As such, a state's urbanization should be calculated as follows:

Urbanization = (WorkforceScore / 40) + InputGoodScore + OutputGoodScore + ((InputGoodScore + OutputGoodScore) / 5)

This will hopefully yield a ratio of roughly 8:1 between a modern motor industry and a wheat farm, which aligns well with the historical difference in their contribution to urbanization. It also means that upgrading production methods will naturally increase urbanization without needing to add any special-case logic, because input good score and the output good score rise alongside increasingly urban job types.

# Research

To allow for increased flexibility in playstyles and to create a more realistic simulation of how innovation is achieved in the cold war era and beyond, as well as to represent the difference between countries in the ability to fund innovation and be the leading innovating countries, how research works needs to be adjusted.

The first would be to remove the ahead of time penalty that gets applied when a country tries to research a technology in a new era while there are still technologies in the current/previous eras that have yet to be resarched. By setting this to 0 to remove the penalty, we can instead make use of the eras to assign how much innovation each set of technology should cost to research, increasing the innovation cost for each era in a non-linear increase. If a country wants to focus on achieving an advanced innovation at the cost of falling behind in other areas, such as Taiwan investing into semiconductor fabs, then that shouldn't be prohibited; this would also allow the superpowers and great powers to stay ahead technologically in front of the other countries as they would be able to afford the increasing cost to generate enough innovation to keep up with the increasing innovation costs of higher era technologies.

Innovation should be generated in the following places:
- Universities
- Company Headquarters (not regional headquarters)

Universities should be the default place to generate innovation; while Company Headquarters should generate a set amount of innovation per level when the company has unlocked their prosperity bonus, rewarding the development of profitable and large companies.
