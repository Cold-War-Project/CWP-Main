## 2. The line on 1 January 1946 and the two zones

### 2.1 Was it strictly the parallel?

- **Yes, in law: 38 degrees north, nothing else** (C). General Order No. 1 named the parallel; no agreed adjustment existed on 1 Jan 1946 or later. The line followed no river, ridge or county boundary. The US Army official history counts what it cut: more than 75 streams and 12 rivers, 181 cart tracks, 104 country roads, 15 provincial roads, 8 highways and 6 north-south railway lines (P: Appleman, *South to the Naktong*; wording not fetched).
- **Soviet troops south of the line, briefly** (C in substance, P for dates): the 25th Army reached Kaesong (and reportedly other points just south of the parallel) in late Aug 1945 and withdrew north of the line when US forces arrived in September.
- **Sealing of the line** (C, WP8): from autumn 1945 the Soviet command stopped mail, rail traffic and coal shipments across the parallel and obstructed power transmission; it refused talks on these through the autumn. Paragraph 4 of the Moscow decision led to a US-Soviet command conference in Seoul (16 Jan-5 Feb 1946) that settled only minor matters such as mail exchange (P).
- **Local practice** (U): accounts of ad hoc arrangements (US road convoys to Ongjin through Soviet-held Haeju; marker posts and guard huts; split villages) could not be confirmed in a fetched source. Treat as texture only if a source is found.
