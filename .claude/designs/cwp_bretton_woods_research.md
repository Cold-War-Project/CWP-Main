# Research brief: the Bretton Woods system and what followed, 1944-2016

Research sub-agent, 2026-11-21. Certainty: **V** verified in a source read in this session (fetched page, downloaded paper, or a search result quoting an institutional source); **P** probable (recalled, or one weak or unread source); **X** the claim as given is wrong (correction given). A row can carry two marks: the first for the main claim, the second for a detail.

Abbreviations for sources actually read:
- **Bordo93** = M. Bordo, "The Bretton Woods International Monetary System: A Historical Overview", ch. 1 of Bordo & Eichengreen (eds), *A Retrospective on the Bretton Woods System* (NBER, 1993). Main secondary source for sections A-C.

Weak spots: Horsefield / de Vries (the IMF's official histories to 1978), Steil, Eichengreen's *Globalizing Capital*, Skidelsky and Schenk's 1998 article were **not** read directly; they appear only as cited by the sources above. ## 2. Corrections that change the design

1. **No warship fetched the gold (row 15).** The cruiser was de Gaulle's idea and was dropped; the gold flew Air France. An event can use the cruiser as the rejected option.
2. **"Exorbitant privilege" is Giscard's phrase, not de Gaulle's.** Giscard also opposed the gold campaign's sharper forms. In text, give the phrase to the finance minister.
3. **The British "gold demand" of August 1971 was a request for exchange-rate cover**, agreed for $750m. A trigger event should read "London asks for a guarantee on its dollars", with "Washington hears a run on gold" as the effect.
4. **Connally's line has no dated source.** Use it as a reported saying ("as Connally is said to have told the Europeans"), never with a date or place.
5. **The USSR did answer, once** (Molotov, end of Dec 1945: not now, more study). It never gave reasons. The membership window ran to the end of 1946. A Soviet-ratification branch is historically grounded: Stalin's advisers were for it.
6. **Two firsts in May 1947**: the IMF's first drawing (France, 8 May) and the World Bank's first loan (France, $250m, 9 May).
7. **When dollars outran gold depends on which dollars.** All foreign claims: about 1960. Official claims alone: 1964-66. Model them as two thresholds.
8. **The eurodollar market starts in 1955 with a British clearing bank.** The Soviet banks belong to it as early depositors, not as proven inventors.
9. **No petrodollar treaty.** What exists is a secret arrangement on Saudi purchases of US Treasury debt and a public cooperation commission.
10. **Suez:** conditional US support, not a veto (as in the Suez brief).
11. **The Smithsonian did not reopen the gold window.** The new $38 price was one at which the US would not sell.

## 7. Things the designer should know

- **Three clocks ran at once.** Liquidity (not enough gold), confidence (dollars against gold) and adjustment (nobody changes parity). Bordo93 organises the whole story around them, and they map onto separate model variables.
- **The system as designed barely existed.** Full operation ran only from 1959 to 1968 (some say 1971). Before 1959 Europe lived on controls, Marshall aid and the EPU; after March 1968 the dollar was convertible in name only.
- **Parities froze.** Meant to be adjustable, they became almost fixed after 1949, because every change brought loss of face and a one-way bet for speculators. The model should make parity changes costly and late.
- **The United States could not devalue on its own.** Any change in the dollar's gold price would be matched by others, which is why Washington pushed surplus countries to revalue and in 1971 used the surcharge as a lever.
- **Who ran it:** from 1961 the Group of Ten, the Basel central bankers and the New York Fed's swap network, more than the IMF. Under Nixon control moved to the Treasury (Connally, Volcker).
- **France was inside while attacking.** It stayed in the Gold Pool until June 1967 and its conversions stopped in 1966; May 1968 then turned France into a deficit country that needed American help.
- **The socialist states** were inside at the start (Poland, Czechoslovakia, Yugoslavia, Cuba after 1959 as an inherited member) and left one by one. Romania's entry in 1972 hardened Moscow against others following.
- **One-sided sources met:** bullion dealers' sites (BullionStar, BullionVault, Money Metals, USAGold) present 1965-71 as vindication of gold; they were used only for documents they quote. Mises Institute pages likewise. FRUS and Fed pages speak for US institutions; Avaro's draft is unpublished.
- **Still to read for event text:** Horsefield and de Vries (IMF official histories), Coombs's *The Arena of International Finance* (1976), Solomon's *The International Monetary System 1945-1981*, Volcker & Gyohten's *Changing Fortunes* (1992), Steil (2013), Skidelsky vol. 3, Schenk's *The Decline of Sterling* (2010), Gavin's *Gold, Dollars, and Power* (2004), James's *International Monetary Cooperation since Bretton Woods* (1996).
