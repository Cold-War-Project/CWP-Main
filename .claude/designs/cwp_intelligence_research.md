# Research brief: espionage and intelligence, 1945-2020 (companion to cwp_intelligence.md)

Research sub-agent, 2026-11-22. Checked against the design at rev 0.

**Certainty marks**

| Mark | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **C** | confirmed this session in two or more good sources, or in one official history or primary document |
| **C\*** | a date of record that the standard reference works give identically; not re-fetched this session. Safe for design, re-check before it is quoted in event text |
| **P** | probable: one good source, or sources differ on a detail (the difference is stated). **P (nf)** = from the standard literature, not fetched this session |
| **D** | disputed among historians, or doubtful. The dispute is stated |

Source numbers in square brackets refer to section 8. WP = English Wikipedia, used as a pointer to the scholarship it cites.

**Covered:** every statement marked [history?] in the design (sections 5.2, 8.4, 10, 11, 13, 14.1, 14.2, 15); the services of 26 countries on 1 January 1946 (27 rows: China twice); penetrations in place on that date; stations in 1946; motives, collection methods and exposure consequences; fifteen further affairs.

**Not covered, or covered thinly:** no archive was visited and no book was read in full: the official histories (Jeffery on MI6, Andrew on MI5, Horner on ASIO), Andrew and Mitrokhin, Haynes, Klehr and Vassiliev, and Holloway are cited from memory of their contents or through web copies and reviews. Sources in Russian, Chinese, Arabic, Turkish, Japanese and the East European languages were read only through English summaries, with a few exceptions (Polish IPN, Italian, Portuguese, German pages). Soviet residency strengths in 1946 and the smaller countries' services (section 3) rest on thinner evidence than the US, UK and Canadian material. Section 9 lists what could not be confirmed.

**Living people.** Named only where a court, an official inquiry or a government finding is the source, and flagged *(living)*. Several people the design might expect to be alive are dead: Oleg Gordievsky (died 4 March 2025) [22], Aldrich Ames (died in federal prison, 5 January 2026) [21], Robert Hanssen (died 5 June 2023, C\*), George Blake (died 26 December 2020, C\*), A.Q. Khan (died 10 October 2021, C\*), Dieter Gerhardt (died July 2026) [78].

## 6. Mechanism facts

### 6.1 Why people spied

| Period | What the record shows | Mark | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1930s-1940s | Conviction dominates among Western sources of the USSR: anti-fascism, the Depression, the wartime alliance. Recruitment through party circles and universities; several of the best sources volunteered. Little or no pay | C\* | [13][14] |
| Late 1940s-1950s | The pool dries up: the Nazi-Soviet pact, defections, security vetting, 1956. Soviet services turn to money, to compromise (sexual entrapment, blackmail), and to people already inside by accident of war or captivity | P (nf) | |
| 1960s-1970s | East German "Romeo" operations against secretaries in Bonn; Americans who sell (Walker from 1967-68). In the other direction, Soviet-bloc officers begin to volunteer from disillusion (Penkovsky 1960-62; Kuklinski 1972-81; Gordievsky from 1974) | P | [84] |
| 1980s | Among Americans who began spying in the 1980s, money alone was the motive in 74 per cent of cases (Defense Department study, as reported). Ames, Pelton, Howard. Disgruntlement at work is the usual companion of money | P | [18] |
| 1990-2007 | The same study: of 37 Americans who began after 1990, two-thirds volunteered; 80 per cent received no payment; "divided loyalties" (ties to another country or cause) was the sole motive in 57 per cent, up from about 20 per cent before; money and coercion in decline. Over the whole period 1947-2007: 173 cases | C (as reported in two summaries) | [18] |
| Throughout | Coercion is rare as a main motive and produces poor agents. Ego and grievance run through nearly every case as secondary motives. The trade's own shorthand is "MICE": money, ideology, compromise, ego (P, nf) | P | |

Design reading: the three channels are sound. Add that **Conviction can point at the spying country's enemy rather than at its own ideology** (anti-Soviet conviction inside the bloc), and that after about 1990 **shared culture or origin** (already in the Access table as a condition) becomes a channel in its own right.
